# 8 · Analytics basics

> **In one sentence:** read a few official numbers in pairs (was it shown, was it clicked, did people stay, did they come back) and use them to choose one change, not to grade yourself.

## Where to look

YouTube Studio → **Analytics** (channel), or **Content** → a video → **Analytics**. Tabs and names change; YouTube says it began rolling out an updated Studio in July 2026 ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11962757?hl=en)). Use the official tool only. If a third-party dashboard disagrees, believe Studio.

## The metrics, with official definitions

| Metric | What it means (official) | Read it with |
|---|---|---|
| **Impressions** | Times your thumbnail was shown on YouTube as a registered impression ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?hl=en)) | CTR |
| **Impressions click-through rate (CTR)** | How often viewers watched after seeing a registered impression ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154?hl=en)) | AVD, traffic source |
| **Views** | Since November 2026, counted the moment any video starts to play (Shorts, long-form and live), and the public count includes an estimate of people watching together on a TV ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?hl=en)) | Engaged views |
| **Engaged views** | Views where someone stayed past the first seconds, not counting loops or co-views ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?hl=en)) | Views |
| **Average view duration (AVD)** | Average minutes watched among those who stayed to watch ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en)) | Video length, retention graph |
| **Average percentage viewed** | Average share of the video watched among those who stayed ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?hl=en)) | AVD |
| **Returning viewers** | People who had watched your channel before and came back in the period ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314416?hl=en)) | New viewers |
| **New / casual / regular viewers** | First time in the period / watched at least monthly for 1–5 of the last 12 months / at least monthly for more than 6 ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10246996?hl=en-GB)) | Monthly audience |
| **Monthly audience** | Estimated unique viewers in the last 28 days ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314416?hl=en)) | Subscribers |
| **Traffic sources** | How viewers found the video: browse features (Home, Subscriptions…), Shorts feed, suggested videos, YouTube search, channel pages, external sites and apps ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?hl=en); [reach](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314355?hl=en)) | CTR |

**What changed in November 2026.** YouTube now adds **co-views** (its estimate of several people watching one TV) to public view counts, and counts a view the moment a video starts to play. YouTube says this does not change YPP earnings or eligibility, which still use engaged Shorts views, engaged watch hours, qualified Shorts views and qualified watch hours ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?hl=en)). So a view count from before November 2026 and one from after are not directly comparable. The same page lists a **co-view rate** metric: divide total views by it to get views without co-viewers.

## CTR: what YouTube says is normal

From the [Impressions & CTR FAQs](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154?hl=en):

- “Half of all channels and videos on YouTube have an impressions CTR that can range between **2% and 10%**.”
- New channels or videos (less than a week old) and videos with fewer than 100 views can see an even wider range.
- Lots of impressions (for example from Home) usually means a **lower** CTR. Traffic mostly from your channel page usually means a higher one.
- Do not decide on too little data, do not chase small changes, and compare videos over the long term.
- High CTR with low AVD and lower-than-expected impressions is YouTube’s description of clickbait.

YouTube’s title tips add one more: for general audiences, look at CTR from **Home** and **Suggested** in the first 24 hours, on videos with above-average impressions; for subscribers, CTR from the **Subscriptions** feed ([Thumbnail & title tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300?hl=en)). A new channel may not have enough impressions for that to mean much yet.

## Returning viewers, and why subscribers are not the whole story

YouTube says the subscriber count “is not the most accurate way to estimate the size of your active audience,” because people subscribe to many channels and stop watching some. It points to **monthly audience** instead ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10246996?hl=en-GB)). To grow regular viewers, its suggestions include uploading consistent content on similar topics or in a familiar format, and using posts and comments (same page). The page also notes that audience segments “do not affect reach or monetisation.”

## Pair the numbers, then make one decision

| What you see | A likely reading | One thing to try |
|---|---|---|
| Few impressions | YouTube has not found many people to show it to yet, or it is new | Wait. Make the next video. Check the title is searchable |
| Impressions fine, low CTR | The packaging did not appeal to the people it was shown to | New title or thumbnail on the next video (or A/B test, [chapter 5](05-titles-and-thumbnails.md)) |
| Good CTR, early drop in retention | The opening did not match the promise | Fix the first 30 seconds ([chapter 6](06-hooks-and-retention.md)) |
| Good start, dip in the middle | One section lost people | Watch that moment; cut similar sections next time |
| Mostly new viewers, few returning | People came for one answer | Normal for how-to. Link related videos, end screens, a series |

These readings are house rules for beginners. They are not YouTube’s diagnosis.

## How often to look

- **48 hours:** a first look at one video. Write the promise *before* you open Studio.
- **7 days:** a second look.
- **28 days:** a channel review, using monthly audience and returning viewers.

These intervals are **house rules**. YouTube itself warns against checking CTR immediately after upload ([Impressions & CTR FAQs](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154?hl=en)).

## Maya

After her fourth long video, Maya does a 28-day review on the [analytics sheet](templates/analytics-review.md). The numbers below are **invented for teaching**, not real data:

- CTR on the four long videos: between 3% and 6%. Inside YouTube’s “half of all channels” range, so she stops worrying about it.
- Traffic: mostly YouTube Search for the onion video; External (her Instagram link) for the first one.
- Average percentage viewed is highest on the video that opens on the pan.
- Returning viewers: a handful. Most viewers are new, which YouTube’s Help says is common for how-to channels.

Her one decision: make the next video part of a series (“one-pan dinners, week 2”) and link it from the end screen of the onion video.
