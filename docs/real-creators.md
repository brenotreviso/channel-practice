# Real creators

Three real YouTube creators are used as worked examples in this guide. We chose people who have explained their own process in public, on video, so every point can be traced to something they said. These are craft examples, not endorsements. We do not quote their subscriber or view counts, because those change and we did not treat third-party counters as official. Where they gave numbers about their own work, we say so and do not use them as benchmarks.

Links and transcripts were checked on **22 November 2026**.

## Internet Shaquille: planning and scripting a long video

**Who:** a home-cooking YouTuber ([official site](https://internetshaquille.com/); [YouTube @internetshaquille](https://www.youtube.com/@internetshaquille)). He was also a craft example in *The Content Practice*.

**Source:** [“How to Make an Internet Shaquille Video”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aBUy0z5HNlY), 16 January 2023, 38:50, on his second channel, Extranet Shaquille. The video’s description calls it “my lesson on video-based instructional design.” His official site links that channel as “Second Channel”.

**Why him:** he teaches long-form as instruction, which suits a beginner explaining something they know. His points are concrete and testable on your next video.

**What we use** ([chapter 4](04-first-long-video.md), [chapter 9](09-cadence.md), [chapter 10](10-community.md)):

- Identify the learner and write a learner outcome first.
- Write a script, edit it ruthlessly, write in your natural voice, read it aloud several times.
- Resolve the title’s question early, then build and resolve new tension.
- Do not introduce the video several times (“saying hi twice”).
- Spend real time on the ending (his own estimate: about 40% of writing time).
- Get a microphone before a better camera (his strong opinion).
- Distinguish asks that serve you from asks that serve the viewer.

## Derek Muller (Veritasium): titles and thumbnails

**Who:** the creator of the science channel [Veritasium](https://www.youtube.com/@veritasium). The video credits say it was written by Derek Muller.

**Source:** [“Clickbait is Unreasonably Effective”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S2xHZPH5Sng), 17 August 2021, 19:25.

**Why him:** a creator who says he was bad at packaging, explaining what changed, and drawing a clear line between enticing and misleading. It speaks directly to the beginner’s fear that a good thumbnail is “selling out”.

**What we use** ([chapter 5](05-titles-and-thumbnails.md)):

- The “Strange Applications of the Magnus Effect” versus “Basketball Dropped From Dam” story.
- “Legitbait” (enticing and true) versus misleading packaging.
- Packaging as “at least half the job”; “no one true title and thumbnail.”
- Changing packaging after release while keeping it accurate.

**Limits:** the video is from 2021, is sponsored (the sponsor segment is not used), and describes a very large channel with a team.

## Jenny Hoyos: Shorts hooks and ideas

**Who:** a Shorts creator known for low-budget food videos ([YouTube @JennyHoyos](https://www.youtube.com/@JennyHoyos)).

**Source:** [“Shorts Genius Shares Everything She Knows (Jenny Hoyos Interview)”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=As7abwNhG7Y), Jay Clouse’s channel, 9 October 2023, 38:13. This is a podcast interview on someone else’s channel, so it is her spoken account, not a written method.

**Also:** [“A deep conversation about Shorts with Jenny Hoyos and Todd Sherman”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_tWy-_otnUc), YouTube’s Creator Insider channel, 9 December 2024, summarised on the [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shorts-deep-dive/) (28 January 2025). Used for the one-second hook and making the hook visual.

**Why her:** Shorts are half of this guide, and she explains her hooks, framing and idea process in plain terms. Her food content sits close to Maya’s.

**What we use** ([chapter 3](03-niche-and-ideas.md), [chapter 6](06-hooks-and-retention.md), [chapter 7](07-shorts-and-long-form.md)):

- You have about one second; the hook is the first frame, ideally a visual shock (Creator Insider).
- A Shorts hook should work as a long-form title and thumbnail: visual, clear without sound, simple (podcast interview).
- Consistent first-frame framing for a series.
- Sketching hooks; learning by imitating hooks, then developing her own.
- Ideas from “living it”; a long list of ideas, few made.
- A good Short is one you watch to the end and rewatch.

**Limits:** her retention and swipe-rate figures are self-reported from a very large channel in 2023, and her readability target is her own method. We mark both as **not benchmarks** and **unverified**.
