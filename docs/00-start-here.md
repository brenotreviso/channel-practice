# 0 · Start here: your first long video and your first Short

> **In one sentence:** this week you will set up a channel, upload one long video of 4–6 minutes and one Short made from it, and look at them once after 48 hours, without deciding that you are “good” or “bad” at YouTube.

You do not need a camera, a studio, or a perfect niche. You need one useful idea, a short script, a quiet room and your phone.

## New words

| Word | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| **Channel** | Your public presence on YouTube. You need one to upload, comment or make playlists ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1646861?hl=en)). |
| **Long-form video** | A regular YouTube video, usually horizontal (16:9), watched on the watch page. In this guide, “long” can mean four minutes. |
| **Short** | A square or vertical video of up to three minutes, watched mostly in the Shorts feed ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)). |
| **Thumbnail** | The still image people see before they click a long video. |
| **Title** | The line of text next to the thumbnail. Up to 100 characters ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/57407?hl=en)). |
| **Hook** | The first moment that tells a viewer why to stay. |
| **Retention** | How much of the video people watched. |
| **YouTube Studio** | YouTube’s official dashboard for uploading, editing and analytics. |

More in the [Glossary](glossary.md).

| Thing | Why |
|---|---|

| A phone that films video | Every exercise assumes this |

## The 7-day path

Do these in order. If a day slips, do not restart the week. Pick up the next step.

### Day 1 · Make the channel and verify your phone

- Create the channel: in YouTube, open **Settings**, then **Add or manage channel(s)**, then **Create a channel**. Choose a picture, a name and a **handle** (your @name) ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1646861?hl=en)). If you want a channel name that is not your Google Account name, or more than one manager later, create it as a **Brand Account** channel (same page).
- Verify your phone number. Without it you cannot upload a custom thumbnail or a video longer than 15 minutes ([feature access](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9890437?hl=en); [longer videos](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/71673?hl=en)).
- Write a two-line channel description: who it is for, and what they get.

[Channel setup checklist](checklists/channel-setup.md). More in [chapter 2](02-channel-setup.md).

### Day 2 · Pick one question someone actually asks

Write three lines:

- **Viewer:** one kind of person (not “everyone”).
- **Question:** one thing they would type into YouTube search, or ask you in a comment.
- **Promise:** what they will be able to do after watching. Finish the sentence “After this video, you will be able to…”.

That last line is borrowed from instructional design. Internet Shaquille, a home-cooking YouTuber, calls tying every video to a learner outcome the first thing to get right ([his process video, 2023](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aBUy0z5HNlY)). See [Real creators](real-creators.md).

### Day 3 · Write a 4–6 minute script

Use the [video plan](templates/video-plan.md) and the [long-video script](templates/long-video-script.md). The shape:

1. **Answer early (first 30 seconds).** Say what the video gives them and start giving it. YouTube Studio reports how many viewers are still watching after 30 seconds, and links a weak number to a mismatch with the title and thumbnail ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en)).
2. **Three steps or three parts.** One idea each.
3. **One “but what about…”** A doubt your viewer has, answered.
4. **A real ending.** Not “that’s it, bye”. Say what they can now do.

Read it out loud. Cut any sentence that does not serve the promise. A 4–6 minute target is a **house rule** for a first video, not a YouTube rule.

### Day 4 · Film it

- Phone sideways (horizontal) for the long video.
- Face the window, not your back to it.
- Record sound close to your mouth if you can: a clip-on mic, or the phone nearer than feels natural.
- While you film, also take three or four still photos you could use as a thumbnail. YouTube suggests taking pictures during the shoot for this ([Thumbnail & title tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300?hl=en)).
- Film one 20–40 second vertical clip of the single most useful moment. That is tomorrow’s Short.

### Day 5 · Edit, title, thumbnail, upload

- Cut dead air, the false starts, and any second introduction.
- **Title:** accurate and short, with the important words first ([Thumbnail & title tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300?hl=en)). Under 100 characters is the hard limit ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/57407?hl=en)).
- **Thumbnail:** one of your stills, one clear subject, a few large words at most. Custom thumbnails need the phone verification from day 1 ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72431?hl=en)).
- **Description:** the first few lines say what the video is, because that is what people see before “more” ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12948449?hl=en)).
- **Audience:** answer the “made for kids” question honestly; it is required ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076?hl=en)).

Walk the [title and thumbnail checklist](checklists/title-and-thumbnail.md) and [before you upload](checklists/before-you-upload.md), then publish.

### Day 6 · Upload one Short, then reply, don’t refresh

- Edit the vertical clip into a Short: one point, captions on screen, the answer near the start. Upload it from the YouTube app (**Create**, then **Short**) or Studio ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10059070?hl=en)).
- If you have advanced features, add your long video as the Short’s **related video**, so a viewer can tap through ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14075157?hl=en)). If you do not have them yet, skip it. That is normal for a new channel.
- Reply to any comment like a person. Do not open analytics all day.

### Day 7 · A 48-hour review, then one next step

Open YouTube Studio for the long video. Use the [48-hour review checklist](checklists/review-48h.md) and the [analytics sheet](templates/analytics-review.md). Write four lines:

1. What I promised.
2. Where people left (the retention graph, if it has enough views to show one).
3. What I would change once.
4. The next video (one sentence).

YouTube itself warns against judging click-through rate straight after upload or on small numbers ([Impressions & CTR FAQs](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154?hl=en)). Do **not** delete the video because the numbers are small. Quiet early numbers are a common reason people quit ([r/NewTubers](https://www.reddit.com/r/NewTubers/comments/1p26dtm/how_do_you_keep_going_when_your_videos_get_almost/), an anecdote).

## Maya’s first week on YouTube

Maya already knows how to make a 30-second Reel. What frightens her is six minutes. She makes the channel under her own name, verifies her phone, and picks the question her Instagram comments ask most: *how do I stop the onions burning?* Her promise: “After this, you can cook the one-pan tomato dinner without the bitter taste.” Her first script is twelve minutes long when she reads it aloud. She cuts the long story about her week, the shopping list and a second recipe, and lands at five and a half, but she keeps a 45-second hello at the top. She films at the kitchen table, takes four stills of the pan, and films one vertical clip of the low-heat trick. The long video goes up on Friday; the Short on Saturday. She does not have advanced features yet, so the Short has no related video. After 48 hours she writes: “Promise kept. Lots of people left by 0:40, still in my ‘hi, welcome’ intro, before the pan was even on screen. Next time: start with the pan. Next video: the same dinner for one, in ten minutes.”

That is a successful first week. The metric is “I uploaded and I reviewed,” not “it took off.”
