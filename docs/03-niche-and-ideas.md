# 3 · Niche and ideas

> **In one sentence:** pick one kind of viewer and one theme for your long videos, then turn real questions into videos that each keep one promise.

## New words

| Word | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| **Niche** | The theme and the viewer your channel is for. Narrow enough that a stranger can tell what they will get. |
| **Pillar** | A repeating theme you can make many videos about (from *The Content Practice*). |
| **Searchable title** | A title that says plainly what the video is, for people searching ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300?hl=en)). |
| **Intriguing title** | A title that sparks curiosity, for people browsing who were not looking for the topic (same page). |
| **Promise** | What the viewer gets by the end. One per video. |

## One viewer, one theme, to start

YouTube’s Help on viewer types says that channels which post similar formats or topics tend to see more viewers come back, and that a mostly-new audience can mean people care about one topic or video more than the channel ([New, casual & regular viewer tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13615784?hl=en-GB)). It also notes that “how-to” channels often see many new viewers who leave once they have their answer (same page). Neither is bad. It helps to know which you are building.

If you came from *The Content Practice*, you already have pillars. For long-form, **start with one pillar**. It is easier to plan, easier to title, and easier for a viewer to know what to expect next. Other pillars can live in Shorts while you learn.

## Where ideas come from

1. **Questions you are already asked.** Comments on your other accounts, messages, things friends ask. These are the best ideas a beginner has, because a real person wants the answer.
2. **YouTube search.** Type the start of a question in the YouTube search box and read the predictions. Search is a clue, not an order.
3. **YouTube Studio research.** Studio has a Research / Trends area to explore what viewers search for on YouTube ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11962757?hl=en)); YouTube also suggests using research insights and the Audience tab’s “other videos your audience watched” for title and thumbnail ideas ([Thumbnail & title tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300?hl=en)). Expect little data until you have an audience. The page notes Studio is being redesigned from July 2026, so names may differ.
4. **Your own life.** Jenny Hoyos, a Shorts creator known for low-budget food videos, says her best ideas “come from just living it” and noticing when something is a video ([interview with Jay Clouse, 2023](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=As7abwNhG7Y)). In the same interview she says she keeps a document of about a thousand ideas and makes far fewer. That is her practice, not a target.

## Turn a question into a video

| Step | Ask | Example (Maya) |
|---|---|---|
| Question | What exactly did they ask? | “Why does my tomato pan taste bitter?” |
| Viewer | Who asked, and what do they already know? | Tired after work, can cook a little |
| Promise | After this video, they will be able to… | Cook the pan without the bitter taste |
| Proof | What will they see that shows it works? | The onions at 3 minutes vs 12 minutes |
| Title type | Searchable or intriguing? | Searchable first: people type this |

Fill this in on the [video plan](templates/video-plan.md).

## Long or short?

Ask what the promise needs. A single fix, a reveal or a before-and-after can be a Short. A process with steps, a comparison, or a “why” that needs an example is usually a long video. [Chapter 7](07-shorts-and-long-form.md) covers using both.

## Maya

Maya’s four pillars stay, but long-form starts with **weeknight cooking** only. She opens the last month of comments on her Reels and copies every question into a list. Twelve are about the same dinner: timings, bitterness, swaps, leftovers. That list is her first four long videos. Mending stays on Shorts for now, and so do the canal walks.

## Exercise

Write your viewer and your one long-form theme. Then list ten real questions in that theme, from comments, friends, or YouTube search predictions. Circle the three you could answer best on camera.
