# 1 · How YouTube finds viewers

> **In one sentence:** YouTube tries to show each viewer the videos they will find worth their time, in a few different places, and your job is to make a video that is worth it for one kind of person and to say clearly what it is.

## New words

| Word | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| **Recommendation system** | Software that picks which videos to show each viewer. |
| **Home** | The page you see when you open YouTube: a mix of personalised recommendations, subscriptions and news ([How YouTube Works](https://www.youtube.com/howyoutubeworks/product-features/recommendations/)). |
| **Up Next / Watch next** | Suggestions shown next to or after the video someone is watching. |
| **Search** | Results for what someone typed. |
| **Shorts feed** | The vertical, swipeable feed for Shorts. |
| **Browse** | Viewers finding you without searching (Home, Up Next, the Shorts feed). |
| **Impression** | Your thumbnail was shown to someone on YouTube. |

## Where viewers come from

YouTube describes recommendations at work in two main places, **Home** and **Up Next**, and search results that weigh **relevance, engagement and quality** differently depending on the search ([How YouTube Works, Recommendations](https://www.youtube.com/howyoutubeworks/product-features/recommendations/)). Shorts are found mainly in the Shorts feed, and also in search, on Home, on your channel page, in Subscriptions and in notifications ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10059070?hl=en)).

For a new channel, two routes matter most:

1. **Search.** Someone types a question. A clear, accurate title and a video that answers it are your best chance.
2. **Browse.** YouTube tries your video on a few viewers. If they click and stay, it may show it to more.

## What YouTube says it is trying to do

From the official page:

- The system tries to “anticipate and meet a user’s needs” with “relevant and satisfying viewing experiences.”
- It uses a viewer’s watching behaviour, likes, dislikes, subscriptions and feedback, **including satisfaction surveys**.
- It also considers the reputation and quality of a channel, and uses external evaluators ([How YouTube Works](https://www.youtube.com/howyoutubeworks/product-features/recommendations/)).

Two Help pages add a practical point. YouTube recommends a video when it is relevant to a viewer and its **average view duration** suggests people find it interesting. A thumbnail that gets clicks but loses viewers quickly is called out as clickbait that is “less likely to get recommended” ([Impressions & CTR FAQs](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154?hl=en)). And YouTube’s own A/B test tool picks the winning title and thumbnail by **watch time**, not clicks ([A/B test titles & thumbnails](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714?hl=en)).

The lesson for a beginner: **the click and the watch are one promise.** The title and thumbnail make it; the video keeps it.

## Your video is compared with everyone else’s

YouTube Help says its systems rank each video “against all the other videos a viewer might watch,” not only against your own channel ([Impressions & CTR FAQs](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154?hl=en)). That is why a video can look good in your own numbers and still get few impressions. It is not a punishment. It is competition for one slot on someone’s screen.

## Shorts and long-form are recommended separately

YouTube says it has built **separate recommendation systems** for Shorts and long-form, because people watch the two differently. The systems “understand cross-format viewing,” but each mainly serves its own format ([YouTube Blog, May 2025](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/debunking-common-myths-about-youtube-shorts/)). [Chapter 7](07-shorts-and-long-form.md) is about what that means for you.

## Other platforms do not carry over

Followers on Instagram or TikTok are not YouTube subscribers, and YouTube does not say it uses them. If people come from a link you share, Studio reports them under **External** traffic ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?hl=en)). That can help a first video. It is not the same as YouTube recommending you.

## What we do not know

YouTube does not publish its ranking formula. Any “the algorithm wants X minutes / Y uploads a week” rule that is not on an official page is **unverified** in this guide. Treat it as someone’s experience, at best.

## Maya

Maya half-expects her Instagram viewers to arrive on day one. A few do, from a link in her bio. Then the views stop. She reads this chapter and changes her goal from “bring my audience over” to “make one video that a stranger who searches *burnt onions bitter* would be glad they clicked.” That goal is in her control.
